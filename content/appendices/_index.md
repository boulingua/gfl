## The set

- **[The Greek alphabet and its sounds](/appendices/alphabet-and-pronunciation/)**
  — the twenty-four letters, their names, their sound values, the digraphs, and
  the transliteration scheme used in Level 0. Available now.
- **Monotonic accentuation** — where the tonos goes, the monosyllables that
  take one, the double accent before a weak pronoun, and capitals.
- **Noun and verb paradigms** — the declension classes by gender and ending,
  and the present/aorist/future paradigms of the verb classes met in the course.
- **Thematic glossary** — Greek headword with its ELOT 743 transliteration and
  an English gloss, grouped by theme and tagged with the unit that introduces it.
- **CEFR self-assessment grid** — the can-do statements of Level 0 to B1 in one
  table, for learners to tick and for teachers to use as a placement aid.
- **Common errors** — the mistakes this course actually produces, with the
  reason behind each: ι/η/υ/ει/οι spelling, the dropped tonos, final sigma,
  gender of nouns in -ος, and αυ/ευ read as spelt.

{{< callout type="note" title="Build status" >}}
Appendices are authored alongside the levels they serve: the alphabet reference
with Level 0, the paradigm tables with A1 and A2, the glossary continuously, and
the self-assessment grid when B1 completes `core` conformance.
{{< /callout >}}

## A note on Cyprus and the dialects

The course teaches Standard Modern Greek (Dimotiki) and nothing else. Cypriot
Greek, the northern vowel-raising dialects and the older Katharevousa register
are real and worth knowing about, but mixing them into the core would produce
variant drift in a beginner's output. They are treated in a cultural note in
the appendix set rather than in the units.
