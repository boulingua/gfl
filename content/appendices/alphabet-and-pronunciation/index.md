This page is the reference behind [Level 0](/level-0/). It states what each
letter and letter-group sounds like, in Standard Modern Greek, monotonic
orthography. It is deliberately a reference and not a lesson: the teaching
sequence, the practice and the audio live in the Level 0 units.

## The twenty-four letters

The column *ELOT* gives the single-letter correspondences of the ELOT 743
transliteration standard, which this course uses for Level 0 scaffolding and
for glossary headwords. *Sound* describes the value in ordinary speech; where a
letter has two values, both are given with the condition that selects them.

| Letter | Name | ELOT | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Α α | άλφα | a | *a* as in *father*, always short |
| Β β | βήτα | v | *v* as in *very* — never *b* |
| Γ γ | γάμα | g | a soft *gh* before α, ο, ω, ου and consonants; like English *y* in *yes* before ε, ι, η, υ, αι, ει, οι |
| Δ δ | δέλτα | d | *th* as in *this* — never *d* |
| Ε ε | έψιλον | e | *e* as in *bed* |
| Ζ ζ | ζήτα | z | *z* as in *zoo* |
| Η η | ήτα | i | *ee* as in *see* |
| Θ θ | θήτα | th | *th* as in *think* |
| Ι ι | γιώτα | i | *ee* as in *see* |
| Κ κ | κάπα | k | *k*; palatalised before ε, ι, η, υ, αι, ει, οι |
| Λ λ | λάμδα | l | *l* as in *let* |
| Μ μ | μι | m | *m* |
| Ν ν | νι | n | *n* |
| Ξ ξ | ξι | x | *ks* as in *box* — one letter, two sounds |
| Ο ο | όμικρον | o | *o* as in *pot* |
| Π π | πι | p | *p* |
| Ρ ρ | ρο | r | a tapped *r*, as in Italian *caro* |
| Σ σ ς | σίγμα | s | *s* as in *see*; *z* before β, γ, δ, μ, ν, ρ, λ (κόσμος) |
| Τ τ | ταυ | t | *t* |
| Υ υ | ύψιλον | y | *ee* as in *see* |
| Φ φ | φι | f | *f* |
| Χ χ | χι | ch | Scottish *loch* before α, ο, ω, ου; German *ich* before ε, ι, η, υ |
| Ψ ψ | ψι | ps | *ps* as in *lapse* — one letter, two sounds |
| Ω ω | ωμέγα | o | *o* as in *pot*, identical to ο |

### The two sigmas

**σ** is used everywhere except at the end of a word; **ς** is used only at the
end. They are the same letter and the same sound: σεισμός has both. Writing
*σεισμοσ* or *ςεισμός* is an error, not a variant.

### Five letters, one sound

**η, ι, υ, ει and οι all represent /i/**, and **ο and ω** both represent /o/.
Reading Greek is therefore easy in one direction and hard in the other: a
written word has one pronunciation, but a heard word may have several possible
spellings. This is a spelling problem, and the course treats it as one — the
distinctions are learned per word, with the vocabulary, not by listening harder.

## Vowel digraphs

Two letters, one vowel sound.

| Digraph | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| αι | *e* as in *bed* | και — *and* |
| ει | *ee* as in *see* | είναι — *is / are* |
| οι | *ee* as in *see* | οικογένεια — *family* |
| ου | *oo* as in *food* | ούζο — *ouzo* |
| υι | *ee* as in *see* | υιοθετώ — *I adopt* (rare) |

**αυ** and **ευ** behave differently: they are a vowel plus a consonant, and
which consonant depends on what follows.

| Digraph | Before a voiced sound or a vowel | Before a voiceless consonant |
|---|---|---|
| αυ | *av* — αύριο (*tomorrow*) | *af* — αυτός (*he*) |
| ευ | *ev* — Ευρώπη (*Europe*) | *ef* — ευχαριστώ (*thank you*) |

## Consonant digraphs

| Digraph | Word-initially | Inside a word | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| μπ | *b* | *mb* | μπίρα (*beer*), λάμπα (*lamp*) |
| ντ | *d* | *nd* | ντομάτα (*tomato*), πέντε (*five*) |
| γκ | *g* | *ng* | γκάζι (*gas*), αγκώνας (*elbow*) |
| γγ | — | *ng* | αγγλικά (*English*) |
| τσ | *ts* | *ts* | τσάι (*tea*) |
| τζ | *dz* | *dz* | τζατζίκι (*tzatziki*) |

γγ never begins a word; μπ, ντ and γκ do, which is why their two readings have
to be learned together rather than as separate rules.

## The tonos

Modern Greek is written **monotonic**: one accent mark, the *tonos*, and one
diacritic, the diaeresis. The polytonic system — three accents and two
breathings — was abolished for general use in 1982 and appears in this course
only where a quoted historical text requires it.

- Every word of **two or more syllables** carries a tonos on its stressed
  syllable: καλημέρα, θάλασσα, ευχαριστώ.
- **Monosyllables take no tonos** — ναι, ποιος, φως — with a small set of
  exceptions that distinguish homographs: **ή** (*or*) against **η** (*the*),
  **πού** (*where?*) against **που** (*that, which*), **πώς** (*how?*) against
  **πως** (*that*).
- A word written **entirely in capitals** takes no tonos: ΑΘΗΝΑ, ΕΞΟΔΟΣ. A word
  with only its first letter capitalised keeps it: Άννα, Έλλη.
- A **second tonos** appears when a word stressed on its third-from-last
  syllable is followed by a weak pronoun: ο δάσκαλος → ο δάσκαλός μου, το
  αυτοκίνητο → το αυτοκίνητό της.

## The diaeresis

Two dots over ι or υ mean *read these two vowels separately* — the digraph is
cancelled: παϊδάκια (*pai-*, not *pe-*), κοροϊδεύω, Μάιος.

The diaeresis is unnecessary when a tonos already sits on the **first** of the
two vowels, because that alone separates them: ρολόι, κορόιδο. When the stress
falls on the **second** vowel of a cancelled digraph, the two marks combine into
one character: **ΐ** and **ΰ** — πρωτεΐνη (*protein*), Μαΐου (*of May*).

## Typing monotonic Greek

On a standard Greek keyboard layout the tonos is the `;` key, pressed before the
vowel: `;` + `α` → ά. The diaeresis is `Shift`+`;` before the vowel, and the
combined ΐ / ΰ is produced by `Shift`+`W` before the vowel. The final sigma ς is
on the `w` key.

Accents are not optional decoration. A dropped tonos is a spelling error in
Greek exactly as a dropped umlaut is in German, and it can change the word:
πότε (*when?*) against ποτέ (*never*), νόμος (*law*) against νομός
(*prefecture*).
