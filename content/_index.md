Greek asks something of a beginner that French or Spanish does not: before the
first greeting can be read, the alphabet has to be learned. This course
therefore begins one step earlier than its sister courses, with a **Level 0**
stage devoted entirely to reading and writing, and only then moves through
A1, A2 and B1.

## The course

`gfl` is the Modern Greek course of the [boulingua](https://github.com/boulingua)
platform: a curriculum-aligned, openly-licensed course for learners in the German
*Gesamtschule* system and for independent adult self-learners. Every unit follows
the same five-step model — **Activate → Input → Practise → Apply → Reflect** — and
carries an answer key, a teacher note, an editable slide deck, a printable
worksheet and native-voice audio.

The variety taught is **Standard Modern Greek (Dimotiki)** in **monotonic**
orthography: one accent, the *tonos*, plus the diaeresis. Neither Katharevousa
nor the polytonic system appears anywhere in the course.

## How the course is put together

- **Level 0 is taught, not assumed.** Reading readiness is a learning objective
  with its own units, audio and worksheets — not a prerequisite. Latin
  transliteration is used as a scaffold inside Level 0 and for glossary
  headwords only; from A1 onward, Greek appears in Greek script.
- **CEFR mapping is declared, not implied.** Each unit's can-do statements map
  to descriptor IDs of the shared
  [curriculum framework](https://github.com/boulingua/curriculum); the course
  declares `core` conformance (A1–B1) and records `no-official-descriptor`
  wherever the Companion Volume leaves a cell empty at these levels.
- **Materials are open formats.** Slide decks ship as `.odp` and PDF, worksheets
  as PDF; both are generated from the shared LaTeX templates and committed to
  the repository, so nothing has to be rebuilt to be used.
- **Sources are open.** Cultural and factual content is drawn from
  public-domain or openly-licensed material only, and cited on the page.
