B1 is the level at which a learner stops reporting facts and starts taking
positions. The grammar of this level — the subjunctive, conditionals, relative
clauses, reported speech — exists to make that possible; the topics are news,
history, work and the city, where opinions are actually held.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## What changes at B1

The sentence gets longer and the speaker gets a stance. Three strands run
through the level:

- **Subordination.** να-clauses, αν-clauses and relative clauses let one
  proposition depend on another. Until they are secure, a learner can only
  produce lists of main clauses, which is why B1 opens with them.
- **Register.** Greek marks the distance between speakers grammatically
  (εσύ / εσείς) and lexically, and the gap between a text message and a formal
  email is wide. B1 makes register an object of study rather than an accident.
- **Length.** Reading texts run to several hundred words, and the writing tasks
  ask for structured opinion, not description. This is where the sociolinguistic
  and pragmatic CEFR scales first carry real weight in the course.

## Exam

B1 closes with the final exam bundle of the `core` course, on the same pattern
as A1 and A2, with the printable paper under `static/downloads/b1/`.

## Curriculum mapping and conformance

B1 completes the course's declared **`core`** conformance (A1–B1). Its can-dos
map to `B1.{DOMAIN}.{SCALE}.{SEQ}` descriptor IDs, and it is the level at which
`SOC` (sociolinguistic appropriateness) and the `PRAG` scales — flexibility,
turntaking, thematic development, coherence — are exercised deliberately rather
than incidentally. Every `implements_id` in `conformance.yml` must resolve
through the framework's ID audit before this level is declared complete.

B2 remains a stretch goal; C1 and C2 are explicitly out of reach for a freely
maintained open course and are not claimed.
